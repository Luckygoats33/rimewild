Oregon's Central Coast is one of the most productive wild fisheries in the Pacific Northwest. From the Siletz to the Siuslaw, these rivers run through dense Coast Range timber, fed by heavy coastal rain and snowmelt that keeps the water cold and the fish moving.

Rimewild operates on four river systems — the Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, and Yaquina. Each river has its own character, its own runs, and its own best conditions. We choose the river based on the day, the season, and the fish. That flexibility is the advantage of fishing with a local guide who knows every piece of water.

Every trip includes all gear, tackle, and on-water instruction. Whether you've never held a rod or you've been fishing for decades, Corey meets you where you are and makes sure you leave knowing more than when you showed up.

Flagship Experience

Winter
Steelhead Drift

The winter steelhead run is the reason Rimewild exists. From December through April, wild steelhead push into the Siletz, Alsea, and surrounding coastal rivers — chrome-bright fish running 8 to 15 pounds, fresh from the Pacific.

This is full-day guided drift boat fishing. You'll float 8–12 miles of river, working proven steelhead water from a stable drift boat platform. Corey rows, you fish. But this isn't passive — you're casting, mending line, reading water, and learning to identify the seams and tailouts where steelhead hold.

By the end of the day, you'll understand why steelhead anglers drive hundreds of miles to fish the Oregon coast — and you'll know how to read this water on your own.

Big Fish Season

Fall Chinook
Salmon

When fall chinook push into Oregon's coastal rivers, the fishing changes entirely. These are big fish — 15 to 40 pounds — running hard upstream on a mission. The Alsea and Siletz both receive strong fall chinook runs from August through November.

We fish chinook with a combination of plug pulling, back-bouncing, and bobber-and-jig techniques depending on water conditions. The approach changes with the river level, clarity, and where the fish are holding.

What to Expect

A Day on
the Water

Here's what a typical full-day fishing trip with Rimewild looks like.

5:30 – 6:00 AM

Meet at the Launch

We meet at the boat ramp. Corey will have the drift boat rigged and ready. Quick introductions, a rundown of the day's plan, river conditions, and what we're targeting. Coffee is a good idea.

6:00 – 6:30 AM

Gear Up & Launch

Rods are rigged for the day's conditions. If you're new, Corey will walk you through casting technique, line management, and what to feel for. Then we push off into the river.

6:30 AM – 12:00 PM

Morning Session

The morning is prime time. We work through the best water, stopping at proven runs, tailouts, and seams. You're casting, working your drift, and reading the river. Corey adjusts technique and rigging based on what the fish are telling us.

12:00 – 12:30 PM

Lunch Break

Pull over at a gravel bar. Eat lunch, stretch your legs, take in the scenery. This is a working break — Corey will often re-rig and adjust for afternoon conditions while you eat.

12:30 – 3:30 PM

Afternoon Session

The afternoon float covers the lower river. Different water, different holding spots. If the morning was productive, we refine what's working. If it was slow, we switch approaches entirely.

3:30 – 4:00 PM

Take Out & Debrief

We pull out at the downstream ramp. Quick debrief — what worked, what we saw, what the fish were doing. If you want to come back and fish on your own, Corey will point you to the best bank access spots.

Species Guide

What You're
After

🎧

Winter Steelhead

Oncorhynchus mykiss

The crown jewel of Central Coast fishing. Wild winter steelhead run 8–15 pounds, chrome-bright from the ocean, and fight with a power that salmon can't match. They're the reason serious anglers drive across the state.

Season: December–April
Average size: 8–15 lbs, up to 20+
Best rivers: Siletz, Alsea
Regulation: Wild fish catch-and-release only
🎧

Chinook Salmon

Oncorhynchus tshawytscha

The biggest fish in the river. Fall chinook run 15–40 pounds, with the occasional 50-pounder showing up in the Siletz and Alsea. They fight differently from steelhead — powerful, dogged runs rather than acrobatic leaps.

Season: August–November
Average size: 15–30 lbs, up to 50+
Best rivers: Alsea, Siletz, Siuslaw
Regulation: Hatchery fish may be retained
🎧

Coho Salmon

Oncorhynchus kisutch

Coho (silver salmon) overlap with the chinook runs in fall. Smaller than chinook but more acrobatic — they jump. Great fight on medium tackle. Their aggressive nature makes them reliable biters.

Season: September–November
Average size: 8–12 lbs
Best rivers: Siletz, Siuslaw
Regulation: Varies by river — check ODFW
🎧

Coastal Cutthroat

Oncorhynchus clarkii clarkii

The resident. Coastal cutthroat live in these rivers year-round, moving between the estuary and upper reaches with the seasons. Aggressive on dry flies and small spinners, they're the most fun fish to catch on light tackle.

Season: May–September
Average size: 10–16 inches, up to 20
Best rivers: Upper Siletz, tributaries
Regulation: Catch-and-release in most areas
Techniques

How We
Fish

We match the technique to the conditions. Here's what you'll learn.

01

Drift Boat Fishing

The primary method. Corey rows the drift boat through productive water while you fish from a stable casting platform. You'll work runs, tailouts, and seams — anywhere steelhead or salmon hold. The boat lets us cover 8–12 miles of river in a day, hitting water that bank anglers can't reach.

02

Bank & Wade Fishing

On foot, in the river. Bank fishing teaches you to read water from shore — identifying current speed, depth changes, structure, and the soft water where fish rest. Wade fishing puts you in the river itself, working through runs at fish level. These are the most transferable skills you'll learn.

03

Plug Pulling & Back-Bouncing

For chinook salmon, we often anchor the boat and pull plugs through productive water, or back-bounce bait along the bottom. These techniques are deadly effective on big fish in deeper, slower water where drift fishing is less productive.

04

Float & Jig / Fly Fishing

Bobber-and-jig is the most versatile steelhead technique — a float suspends a jig at the exact depth where fish are holding. For summer steelhead and cutthroat, we can also swing flies through runs. If you're a fly angler, bring your rod.

Questions

Fishing
FAQ

Do I need to bring my own gear?
No. All rods, reels, tackle, and terminal gear are provided. If you have your own gear and prefer to use it, you're welcome to bring it. Fly anglers — bring your rod and we can swing flies for summer steelhead and cutthroat.
Do I need a fishing license?
Yes. A valid Oregon fishing license is required. Daily, annual, and non-resident licenses are available online through ODFW or at local sporting goods stores in Lincoln City and Newport. We recommend getting your license before the trip day.
What if the weather is terrible?
We fish in rain. Oregon's Central Coast gets 60–80 inches of rain per year, and the fishing is often best during and after rain events when the rivers are rising and fish are moving. Trips are only cancelled for genuinely dangerous conditions — extreme flooding, high winds, or ice.
Can I keep fish?
It depends. Wild steelhead are always catch-and-release. Hatchery steelhead (fin-clipped) and hatchery salmon may be retained per current ODFW regulations, which vary by river and season. Corey will know the current regulations for the water we're fishing.
I've never fished before. Is that okay?
Absolutely. Many of our clients are first-timers. Corey is a patient instructor who starts with the basics — casting, line management, reading water — and adjusts the pace to your learning speed. The Family & Beginner trip is designed specifically for this, but any trip works for beginners.
What should I bring?
Layered clothing (quick-dry, not cotton), a quality rain jacket and pants, warm hat and gloves (winter), polarized sunglasses, lunch and snacks, water, and a camera. We have rain jackets available if you need one.
How far in advance should I book?
Winter steelhead season (December–April) books fastest, especially weekends. We recommend booking 2–4 weeks in advance for peak season. Midweek availability is usually better. Summer and fall trips are more flexible.
What's the cancellation policy?
Full refund with 72+ hours notice. Within 72 hours, we'll work with you to reschedule if possible. Weather cancellations by the guide are always fully refunded or rescheduled.
