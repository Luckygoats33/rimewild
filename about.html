Our Story

Born on
This WaterOregon's Central Coast

Rimewild isn't a franchise. It's one guide, one boat, and a lifetime of local knowledge on the rivers and ridges of the Central Coast.

Corey grew up on Oregon's Central Coast — fishing the Siletz before he could drive, hunting the Coast Range with his family, learning the water the way you learn a language: by immersion, from birth.

He's not a transplant who discovered Oregon on a vacation. He was born here. He knows every riffle, every side channel, every ridgeline where elk cross at first light. That kind of knowledge doesn't come from a guidebook or a season of scouting — it comes from decades on the same water, in the same timber, watching the same systems year after year.

When you book with Rimewild, you're fishing or hunting with someone who treats these rivers and mountains the way a farmer treats his land — with respect earned through daily contact, not romantic distance.

The Mission

Why
Rimewild

What we stand for and how we run every trip.

01

Local Knowledge Over Everything

We fish and hunt the same ground year-round. We don't rotate through a dozen states — we go deep on the Central Coast. That focus means we know what's happening on the water right now, not last month or last year.

02

Teach, Don't Perform

Rimewild trips are built around making you a better angler or hunter, not just putting you on a fish. Every technique is explained. Every decision is shared. You leave knowing more than when you arrived.

03

Small by Design

One guide, one boat, small groups. We don't run a fleet and we don't stack trips. Every client gets full attention on the water. That's not a limitation — it's the point.

04

Conservation First

Wild steelhead are always released. We follow ODFW regulations strictly and advocate for habitat protection on every river we fish. These fisheries exist because people before us cared enough to protect them.

The Land

Oregon's
Central Coast

The rivers, timber, and terrain that make this place unlike anywhere else.

🌊

The Rivers

The Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, and Yaquina — four coastal river systems fed by 60–80 inches of annual rainfall. Cold water, wild fish, and miles of productive runs through dense timber.

🌳

Coast Range Timber

The mountains behind the coast are covered in second-growth Douglas fir, spruce, and alder. This is elk country — thick timber, steep draws, and ridgelines that break into meadows at elevation.

⛰

The Weather

Wet, green, and raw. The Central Coast gets serious rainfall October through April. The upside: rain means rivers rise, fish move, and the hunting pressure drops. The locals fish in the rain.

🐾

The Wildlife

Roosevelt elk, black-tailed deer, black bear, bald eagles, osprey, and every species of Pacific salmon. This corridor is one of the most biologically productive on the West Coast.
