Species Guide

What You're
After

Five fish species and eight game animals across four river systems and the Coast Range. Oregon's Central Coast is one of the most species-rich hunting and fishing regions in the Pacific Northwest.

Fish Species

Winter Steelhead

December – April

The flagship species. Wild winter steelhead in coastal rivers are aggressive, powerful, and chrome-bright. The Siletz and Alsea produce some of the best winter steelhead fishing in Oregon.

8–15 lbs avg
$395 / angler

Fall Chinook

August – November

The king of Pacific salmon. Fall chinook push into coastal rivers with power and size that nothing else matches. They're the reason people book trips months in advance.

15–40 lbs avg
$395 / angler

Summer Steelhead

April – October

Acrobatic, hard-fighting fish in warmer water. Summer steelhead on the Siletz are aggressive hitters that put on aerial displays.

6–12 lbs avg
$375 / angler

Coho Salmon

September – November

Aggressive and willing biters. Coho hit plugs and spinners hard. They're excellent table fare and a perfect species for newer anglers.

8–12 lbs avg

Coastal Cutthroat

May – September

Native trout species. Light tackle, beautiful upper river water, and fish that hit dry flies and small spinners. The best summer fishery on the coast.

1–4 lbs avg
$295 / angler
Big Game

Roosevelt Elk

November (Rifle) • September (Archery)

The largest elk subspecies in North America. Coast Range Roosevelt elk live in dense timber and require patience, conditioning, and local knowledge to hunt successfully.

700–1,100 lbs (bulls)
$2,800 / hunter

Blacktail Deer

October – November

Columbian blacktail are a subspecies of mule deer adapted to dense coastal forest. They're challenging spot-and-stalk quarry in steep, thick terrain.

130–200 lbs avg
$1,800 / hunter

Black Bear

August – December (Fall) • April – May (Spring)

Oregon's Central Coast has a healthy black bear population in the Coast Range. Bears feed heavily on berries and salmon runs, making them predictable near waterways in fall. Spot-and-stalk hunting in thick timber with hounds prohibited in Oregon.

200–400 lbs avg
$2,200 / hunter

Cougar / Mountain Lion

Year-round (with tag)

Oregon has one of the largest cougar populations in the West. Coast Range cats are elusive and rarely seen. Hunting requires patience, sharp glassing skills, and deep knowledge of terrain. Hound hunting prohibited—spot and stalk only.

120–180 lbs avg
Contact for pricing
Upland & Waterfowl

Wild Turkey

April – May (Spring) • November (Fall)

Rio Grande and Merriam's turkeys have established populations on the Central Coast. Spring gobbler season is the prime draw—calling in a strutting tom through dense Oregon timber is world-class.

15–25 lbs avg
$350 / hunter

Upland Birds

September – January

Mountain quail, California quail, ruffed grouse, and band-tailed pigeon in the Coast Range. Walk-up hunting through old growth and second-growth timber with a good dog. Band-tails are a unique coastal specialty.

0.5–1 lb avg
$295 / hunter

Waterfowl

October – January

Pacific Flyway migration brings mallards, pintails, wigeon, teal, and Canada geese through coastal estuaries and river bottoms. The Siletz Bay and Alsea estuary are prime jump-shooting and layout spots.

2–12 lbs avg
$325 / hunter

Coyote & Bobcat

Year-round (no bag limit for coyote)

Predator hunting on the Central Coast. Coyotes respond well to electronic callers in clearcuts and meadows. Bobcats are trapped and called in the Coast Range. Excellent off-season hunting when big game is closed.

20–45 lbs avg
$275 / hunter

Country

Central Oregon Coast rivers from the headwaters of the Coast Range to the tidal estuaries.

Siletz River

67 miles of Coast Range water. The Siletz is Rimewild's home river — winter steelhead runs from December through April make it one of the most productive fisheries on the Oregon coast.

Chinook • Steelhead • Cutthroat

Alsea River

Running from the Coast Range to Waldport, the Alsea delivers strong fall chinook runs and excellent bank access for all skill levels.

Chinook • Coho • Steelhead

Siuslaw River

The Siuslaw winds through dense coastal forest near Florence. Quieter than the Siletz, with fewer boats and more wildlife.

Chinook • Steelhead • Bay Species

Yaquina System

The Yaquina Bay and its tributaries near Newport offer unique tidal fishing alongside traditional river runs.

A Day on the Water

What to
Expect

Every trip is different — weather, water, fish. But here's how a typical winter steelhead day unfolds.

5:30 AM

Meet at the Ramp

We meet at the launch. Coffee's on. We rig rods, talk about the plan, and read the conditions together.

6:00 AM

First Drift

On the water before first light. The first couple hours are the most productive. You're casting immediately.

9:00 AM

Work the Water

We move through 8–12 miles of river, hitting every productive run. You'll learn to read water, spot holding lies, and adjust your presentation.

12:00 PM

Lunch on the River

Pull over, eat, rest, swap stories. You might be processing a fish at this point.

1:00 PM

Afternoon Push

Back on the water. Afternoon light changes the game — different techniques, different spots.

3:30 PM

Final Runs

Last few spots before take-out. Some of the best fishing happens in the last hour.

4:00 PM

Take Out

We clean fish, break down gear, and recap the day. You head back knowing the river better than when you arrived.

Getting Here

Central Oregon
Coast

Rimewild country stretches from Lincoln City south to Florence, centered on the Siletz River corridor.

Straight shot down Highway 18 through the Coast Range. McMinnville to the coast in about 90 minutes. Lincoln City is your gateway.

Conservation

Wild Fish.
Wild Rivers.

Rimewild practices catch-and-release for all wild steelhead. Hatchery fish may be retained per ODFW regulations. We support habitat restoration, river access stewardship, and responsible harvest.

These rivers have sustained people for thousands of years. The fishing is good because the rivers are healthy. Keeping them that way isn't optional — it's the whole point.

We use barbless hooks, rubber-mesh nets, and handle every fish with care. If it's wild, it goes back.

Techniques

How We Fish

⛺

Drift Boat

The workhorse of coastal river fishing. We drift 8–12 miles of river, stopping at every productive run. You fish from a stable platform while Corey rows and positions.

⚐

Back-Bouncing

The go-to technique for winter steelhead. Bouncing bait through steelhead runs is tactile, technical, and deadly effective when done right.

✶

Plug Pulling

Hot-shotting plugs through holding water. The plug works while you hold the rod. When a steelhead hits a plug, you feel it in your chest.

☂

Bank & Wade

Walk the river, read water from shore, and cast to visible fish. The most intimate way to learn a river. No boat between you and the water.

Before
You Go

What to Bring

  • Layered clothing (rain is likely)
  • Quality rain jacket and pants
  • Warm hat and gloves (winter)
  • Polarized sunglasses
  • Lunch, snacks, and water
  • Camera
  • Valid Oregon fishing/hunting license

Oregon Licenses

An Oregon fishing or hunting license is required. Daily, annual, and non-resident licenses are available through ODFW. Licenses can be purchased at local sporting goods stores in Lincoln City and Newport.

Where to Stay

Lincoln City, Newport, Waldport, and Florence all offer lodging. We're happy to recommend places that work well for early-morning departures to the river.
